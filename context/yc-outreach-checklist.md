# YC / Work at a Startup founder outreach — checklist

Use this for the "reach out" box on Work at a Startup, YC company pages, and
any message going directly to a founder at a company under ~30 people.
**Always replace the prefilled opener** ("Hi! My name is X and here's a little
bit about me..."). Leaving it is the clearest signal of a copy-paste.

## What the founder is actually doing
Reading on a phone, maybe 8 seconds, between other things. Not evaluating a
career. Answering one question: **is this person going to save me time or cost
me time?** A small company hiring its first designer is most afraid of hiring
someone who needs managing.

## The five things that get replies

**1. Evidence you thought about their PROBLEM, not their company.**
The single biggest differentiator and almost nobody does it. "I love what
you're building" is noise. Name the hard part they did not advertise.
- Kobalt: compliance agents fail by saying something is fine when it isn't.
- Spur: the hard part isn't test creation, it's whether you believe the report.
- Metriport: you're writing records clinicians act on, so wrong is worse
  than slow.

**2. Something clickable that takes ten seconds.**
Founders do not open PDFs. `yourproject.example.com` beats any portfolio deck.
**This is the user's most underused asset.** It goes in the body and the signature.

**3. Small and concrete beats broad and impressive.**
"I built a validator that rejects runs where the model inflates its own score"
lands. "I have experience designing AI systems" does not. Specific claims are
checkable, and checkable is what makes them credible.
Good specifics in his file: 241 documents / locked dimensions moved zero times ·
48% to 19% with 0% dead ends · reconciles to the cent or it doesn't send ·
$670.72 across 72 line items · six hours to live.

**4. A tiny ask, or none.**
"Happy to walk through any of it" costs nothing. "Can we find 30 minutes this
week?" is homework, and homework gets deferred forever.

**5. Evidence of judgment, ideally including a limit.**
Founders have been burned by people who claim everything. One honest limit makes
everything else believable. **This is the point his drafts keep missing** — both
the Kobalt and Spur first drafts had no limit at all.
- Phrase it as scope, not inability: "the visual identity work I've done is for
  my own product, not for a company."
- "Growing into rather than arriving expert at" ≠ "can't do".
- **Judgement call when the limit is a CORE requirement** (brand at Spur and
  Kobalt is a third of the job). Naming it is riskier there than naming React at
  Metriport or mobile at Popl. Still usually worth it, but it is his call.

## What kills them
- Flattery about funding, investors, logos, or the mission. Founders read that
  all day.
- Resume recap. One line about past employment is the maximum.
- **Length.** Aim for 200 to 250 words. Anything over 280 is long for a phone.
- Anything that reads generated. **Contractions matter more than any other
  single fix** — "it doesn't know" not "it does not know". No em dashes. Name
  real objects (a crumpled Costco receipt, bad light in a car).
- Asking them to do work. "Could you tell me more about the role" is a reply
  that never comes.
- Closing aphorisms. A neat quotable last line reads written, not spoken.

## The honest part about odds
**Most founder replies do not come from cold messages.** They come from warm
signal: a mutual connection, someone who used the product, someone whose public
work the founder already saw. A genuinely good cold message to a tiny company
gets a reply maybe **10 to 25%** of the time. A generic one is near zero, which
is why the average feels like zero.

**What converts cold to warm is having built something publicly that is adjacent
to their problem.** the user has this: a live product with a correctness rule, and
an agent validator. **It is worth more than another ten applications**, and it is
the reason to prefer ten messages like the Spur one over a hundred more forms.

## Sponsorship on YC boards
The visa field usually reads "US citizen/visa only", which means **they do not
sponsor**. If you are on a time-limited work authorization, say what you have
and the date it runs to, rather than letting them guess.

**Do not raise it in a cold first message.** Answer it truthfully the moment a
form field or a person asks, and never deny it:
> I'm on F-1 STEM OPT and authorized to work through June 2028 with no action
> needed from you. After that I'd need an employer to sponsor an H-1B.

## Sequencing
- One message per company. Two people maximum on LinkedIn at a company under
  ~15 people; three is the ceiling.
- **Do not send two founder messages in the same hour.** Space them so either
  reply gets real attention.
- **Follow up once, 5 to 7 days out.** Two lines, no apology, no re-pitch. Most
  people never follow up, which is why it works.

## Filter roles BEFORE writing the message
**Check the years bar first, then the AI language.** Four roles in one week had
a perfect AI-tooling fit and excluded him on years: Newmark 7+, Realtor.com 8+,
Fetch Staff 8+, Calm 7+ with 5+ consumer mobile.

The roles where his AI fluency actually converts pair it with a **3+ or 4+ year
bar**: Spur 3+, Kobalt 4+, Highbeam 4+, TripSuite 3+, Clarasight, Fetch Growth
3+. Those were the strongest scores of the week and it is not a coincidence.
