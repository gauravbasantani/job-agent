# Writing Style Rules

Apply these to every drafted answer, cover letter, and resume bullet. These
are structural/tone rules, not personal data — actual name, title, and
specifics come from `profile/personal-info.md` at generation time.

## Tone
- Plain, conversational language. Write like a competent person explaining
  something to a colleague, not like marketing copy.
- Short sentences over long compound ones.
- No hyphens or em-dashes. Rephrase instead of using them.
- Confident, not embellished. State what happened; let the numbers carry
  the weight.

## Plain language rule (added 2026-08-29, the user's instruction)

This applies to **every message to a human being**: networking messages,
referral asks, thank-you notes, recruiter replies, follow-ups, and screening
answers. the user's words: plain simple language, genuine, no AI tone, no
"hi-fi" words, no jargon.

What that means in practice:

| Do not write | Write |
|---|---|
| "seller-facing experiences that streamline workflows" | "the tools the sales team uses to do their job" |
| "delivering the design behind $5.9M in client sales campaigns" | "that work supported $5.9M in client campaigns" |
| "translating business requirements into user-centered solutions" | "turning what the business needed into screens people could use" |
| "I would value the opportunity to connect" | "I would love 15 minutes to hear how the team works" |
| "introductions like this cost you something" | "I know it takes effort to introduce someone" |
| "so I do not add work for you" | "just so I do this the way that is easiest for you" |
| "I am reaching out regarding" | "I saw the opening for X" |

**Do not announce the structure of your own message.** This is one of the
clearest AI tells and the user flagged it on 2026-08-29. A person says the thing.
A model introduces the thing it is about to say, then says it.

| Announcing (AI) | Just saying it (human) |
|---|---|
| "Short version of why I think I fit:" | "A bit about me." |
| "Here is why I think I am a good fit:" | "Why I think I could be a good fit:" |
| "Some specifics:" | "A few examples:" |
| "To summarize my relevant experience:" | just start with the experience |
| "I wanted to share some context on my background" | "I have spent about 4 years designing..." |

The tell is usually the word **"Here is"** or a noun naming the format
("short version", "quick summary", "some specifics", "a few thoughts on").
A plain label is fine — "Here is the role:" reads naturally because it points at
information, not at the shape of the paragraph that follows. Labelling your own
rhetoric is what sounds machine-written. When in doubt, delete the label and
start with the first real sentence.

Specific things that make a message read as AI-written, all banned:
- Balanced or "clever" sentence pairs written for effect ("X is one thing.
  Y is the harder half.") — one plain sentence beats a crafted one. A single
  natural line like "keeping it clean while everyone is still working in it is
  the part I have spent the most time on" is fine; a polished aphorism is not.
- Triplet lists for rhythm ("clear, scalable, and consistent").
- Words a person would not say out loud in a message: leverage, utilize,
  robust, seamless, holistic, spearheaded, passionate about, deep dive,
  align on, at scale, best-in-class, drive impact.
- Restating the same point in nicer words for emphasis.
- Openers that announce the message ("I wanted to reach out to...").

**The read-aloud test applies to messages too.** If the user would not say the
sentence to this person over coffee, rewrite it.

**The "what does that mean" test.** If the user has to ask what a line means, it
is not plain enough, and the fix is to rewrite it, not to explain it. He asked
this about "so I do not add work for you" on 2026-08-29. Watch for polite hedges
whose logic does not survive a second read: asking someone a question *is*
adding work, so that phrase argues against itself. Say the plain thing instead
("just so I do this the way that is easiest for you").

**Resumes are the exception.** Resume bullets keep the XYZ format and
professional verbs in `context/resume-standards.md`. That is a document format,
not a conversation. Plain language governs everything a human reads as a
message.

## Structural defaults
- Cover letters open with: "Hi I am {{NAME}}, {{TITLE}} with {{ONE_LINE_HOOK}}."
- Never use "reaching out" in a subject line or opener.
- Avoid AI-pattern phrasing: no "I am passionate about leveraging
  synergies," no triplet lists for their own sake ("innovative, dynamic,
  and results-driven"), no unearned superlatives.
- Every screening-question answer should read like it was typed by someone
  who actually knows the company and the role, not copy-pasted. Reference
  something specific to that company when the question invites it.

## Handling repeat questions across applications
Don't reuse an answer verbatim across companies — identical phrasing across
different applications is itself a signal of automation. Keep the
underlying substance (the real story, the real numbers) consistent, but
rephrase and re-anchor it to each specific company and role.
